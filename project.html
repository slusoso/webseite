Reisetagebuch I Auszug (2021)
Essay, 7064 words

The Essay is an Excerpt of my travel diary. An attempt to cross a plateau at 4,200 m ends when a sandstorm makes it physically impossible to continue. This is used as a departure for a sustained meditation on control, complexity, and what it means to be alive inside a system larger than yourself.

The central tension the essay works through is between two fundamentally different ways of understanding existence: one rooted in indigenous Andean thought, specifically Pachamama as the self-generating, nonlinear totality of life, and one rooted in the Western colonial tradition, with its fantasy of omniscient control (the Laplacian Demon), its patriarchal god made in man's image, and its compulsion to subdue and simplify whatever it cannot predict. What Western science has arrived at is a picture of reality far closer to Pachamama than to an central, almighty and patriarchal, Intelligence. The worldview colonial powers imposed by force as self-evident truth, dismissing indigenous cosmologies as primitive superstition, turns out to be the less coherent one. Life, by its nature as a nonlinear complex system, resists exactly the kind of reduction control requires.

The essay ends with geological time: mass extinctions as the condition for new complexity. What is dying is not framed as a tragedy to be reversed so much as a transformation whose terms we might still influence, not by seizing control, but by changing the local rules of interaction that determine how complex systems organise themselves.
